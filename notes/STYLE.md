# STYLE.md — fixed conventions (Fable, 2026-09-24). Provisional items marked (P) until the in-game font test.

- Doctrine: TRANSLATION, ZERO LOCALIZATION. See METHOD.md §0.
- Honorifics: PRESERVED as suffixes: -san, -kun, -chan, -senpai, -sensei, -sama, -dono, -nee, -nii (kin suffixes on names: チガ姉 → Chiga-nee). A drop/change of suffix is an event: flag it in RELATIONS.md. SCOPE (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #6): a suffix is kept only when what precedes it is a personal name, a nickname used as a name, or a title used as a name (先輩, 先生, 部長, with or without the name). On a COMMON NOUN the noun is translated and the suffix is ZERO: お客さん → "the customer" (never "the customer-san"), 皆さん → "everyone", 神様 → "God", 赤ちゃん → "the baby", お巡りさん → "the officer". Where the JP uses the suffixed common noun as ADDRESS, the politeness goes into the noun (sir, ma'am, Mr., miss), not into a suffix. Standalone 先生 as address = "Sensei"; as reference = the profession word (the teacher, the doctor). The qa_check honorific rule covers name + suffix only.
- Standalone kinship words are translated by register, WHEN THE SPEAKER'S OWN RELATIVE IS MEANT: お母さん → Mom, 母さん → Mom, 母 → my mother; お父さん → Dad; おばさん (aunt) → Auntie / Aunt; おじさん → Uncle; お姉ちゃん → Sis (P); お婆さん → Grandma. NON-RELATIVES (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #6): a kinship word addressed to or describing someone who is not the speaker's relative takes what a native English speaker says in that situation, and the choice is logged in DECISIONS — おじさん、これください to a shopkeeper → "Excuse me" / "sir", お姉さん to an unrelated young woman → "miss" / "the lady", おばあさん (stranger) → ma'am / the old lady by context. Spouses using お父さん／お母さん to each other = "Dad" / "Mom" only when the children are the audience; otherwise the name. お姉さん and お姉ちゃん are TWO words with a payoff that forbids merging (CORE C3 Q217): お姉さん gets its own GLOSSARY row before the Q217 files are drafted.
- Name order: GIVEN NAME FIRST, Western order (Haruka Niimura). Decided by the owner 2026-09-24. Historical-style names with の (Minato no Tsunamune) and titles stay as is. Surname-only address (五島 → Goto) is unaffected.
- Romanization: modified Hepburn, NO macrons, long vowels dropped (Goto, Kogori, Kotaro, Ryoji; e.g. Erina Goto). (P) Reason: unknown macron support in the game font. If the font test passes, a one-regex sweep adds macrons. ん before vowel → n' (Shin'ichi). づ → zu, ぢ → ji.
- First-person pronouns: never re-encoded by warping English grammar. When a pronoun does work in a scene (switch, joke, reveal, gender signal), add a translator note (NOTES-TL.md) at the line and translate plainly.
- Speech level: carried by English diction formality per RELATIONS.md row, never by invented quirks. DEFAULT MAPPING when no CAST card overrides (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #14): 乱暴 → high contractions, blunt words, imperatives; タメ口 → normal contractions; 丁寧 (です・ます) → normal contractions PLUS polite words (please, would you, sir / ma'am where the JP addresses) — a です・ます walk-on is not contraction-free ("Yes, we've got it.", not "Yes, we do have it."); 敬語 proper (尊敬語 / 謙譲語) → few contractions and formal words ("allow me", "I would"); fixed keigo formulae → the GLOSSARY rendering. A CAST card's explicit contraction rate always wins. 丁寧 and 敬語 are two levels, not one.
- 「」 → straight double quotes "..."; 『』 → single quotes '...' (P). A JP line that is only 「」 with a line break inside keeps the break.
- Ellipsis: JP …… (2 chars) → "..." once. JP ……… (3+) → "..." once too. Leading ellipsis kept ("...I see"). LIMIT OF THE COLLAPSE (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #17): the collapse stands INSIDE a sentence. A cell that is ONLY ellipsis stays distinct from an empty cell ("..." vs ""). Where one exchange uses ellipsis-only cells of DIFFERENT lengths as a device (「……」 then 「…………」 then 「………………」 in a row of turns), do not collapse them silently: flag the cells and file a QUERIES row.
- Dash: ―― → ― (U+2015), one mark, at the JP's position (never the em dash U+2014; see CHARACTER SET below). A run of three or more ASCII hyphens is a horizontal RULE, not a dash: the source prints separator lines like "-----(中略)-----" with ASCII hyphens, and those are copied as they are (`qa_check` ignores hyphen runs of 3+).
- PUNCTUATION FOLLOWS ENGLISH GRAMMAR, IN EVERY PRINTING (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #11): word-identity rules (CORE B2/B3, "never improve a variant") bind the WORDS, not the punctuation. A question gets its question mark even where that printing of the JP has none (「どこへ行ってるんだろ」 → "Where does all that food go?"): in JP the ？ after んだろ is optional, in English its absence is an error. ……。 → "..." and ――。 at cell end → "―": no extra full stop after "..." or "―". Two variants that differ only in optional JP punctuation (？, 、, 。) get IDENTICAL English plus a DECISIONS row saying so; a JP punctuation difference is carried only when it changes how the line is spoken (行こう vs 行こう？). A refrain keeps its lexical choice (same nouns, same verb) and INFLECTS for its slot ("become a family" → "became a family"); byte-identity is required only where the JP is byte-identical in the same slot (CORE B2 replays).
- ！？ → "!?" ; ？！ → "?!" ; doubled ！！ preserved as "!!".
- Numerals (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #15): DIGITS for clock times, dates, ages, sums of money, measurements, room / route / page numbers, and anything read off a display, a label or a document (15歳 → 15 years old; 0時 → midnight / 12:00 a.m. by context). WORDS for counts under ten in prose and in speech (私たち2人 → "the two of us"; ひとつ息を吐く → "one sigh"). The JP's own choice of Arabic versus kanji numerals (1人 / 一人) is NOT carried; the two rules above decide. Archaic numerals kept where they are labels (CORE A).
- Interjections and response phrases: a GLOSSARY row (pos=interjection) is locked PER FUNCTION, not per string (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #2). The row names the function and its tell — the punctuation after the word, what follows it, who says it (ええ + ？ = surprise, not assent; ほら + something handed over = "Here"; そうだね answering a stated opinion = "You're right"). Per line ask: does the JP word here do the job the row names? YES → paste the row's EN, unvaried. NO → write the natural English for the job it does here and add a GLOSSARY row for that function; file a query only if two rows would then collide. A one-file ruling locks the FUNCTION seen in that file, never the string. Identical-JP-identical-EN (METHOD §4) binds CORE B2 replays, CORE B3 refrains, and lines of about eight characters or more with the same speaker, addressee and function; shorter strings are matched by function, not by bytes. A rendering that a GLOSSARY row prescribes is never an AI-ism: the AI-ism ban is for English with NO JP counterpart.
- SFX, in narration and in speech alike (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #12): render a sound by the English sound-word or descriptive phrase a reader can HEAR (splat, click, a wet slide, a cough). Romanize only a sound the text itself quotes AS a sound — a child's word for a noise, a sound named on screen, one character mimicking another — and then romanize it once with a gloss. The old test ("is the JP a word or pure onomatopoeia?") is a Japanese grammar question and is retired; the test is whether an English reader can hear it. (No italics: plain text only.)
- Text box: width ceiling unknown until measured (P). Do not wrap manually.
- Translator notes (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #10). IN THE LINE, never explain: no added "but", no wink, no gloss inside the sentence. A `[TN: ...]` is allowed in EXACTLY these five cases: (1) the first occurrence of a romanized or untranslated term; (2) a speaker's first dialect line; (3) a pronoun, name-form or register switch that the text itself treats as an event; (4) a pun or homophone kept at its surface meaning where the reader cannot recover the point — state the mechanism in one clause, never restate the punchline; (5) the Latin-script and on-screen-gloss cases named in CORE A. BANNED: register colour the text does not remark on; anything the knowledge fence forbids; anything a Japanese reader would not know either. LENGTH: 12 words maximum. POSITION: after the closing quote, before the speaker tag, at the end of the line. Logged in NOTES-TL.md.
- Notes language: English, JP terms quoted verbatim.
- Source typos (dropped/doubled kana, obvious misconversions): translate the intended text, do NOT reproduce the typo; log each one in QUERIES.md with the exact JP and status=typo. (Fable, 2026-09-24, after chunk 08.)
- CHARACTER SET (Fable, 2026-09-25, from the in-game font test): the script format is CP932 only. Allowed: ASCII plus “ ” ‘ ’ … ― − ～. NOT representable: em dash U+2014, macrons, every accented Latin letter. Dash = ― (U+2015). Ellipsis stays "..." (three ASCII dots). Quotes stay straight ASCII " and '. Names without macrons, as already decided. The (P) items on dash and macrons are now settled.
- ENGLISH VARIETY (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1161): American spelling and vocabulary project-wide (elevator, apartment, first floor = 1階, trash, intercom, center, color). No British forms.
- WHAT THE ALLOWED SET GOVERNS (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #3): it governs text the translator WRITES. A glyph or a space run that CORE A says to KEEP is copied byte for byte and the CORE row WINS over the checker; a HARD line the checker raises on such a glyph is waived with a DECISIONS row naming the CORE row. U+3000 (the full-width space), full-width parentheses （） and the banner brackets 【 】 (CP932 0x8179/0x817A, which stay in English banners by owner ruling) are CP932-legal and are ALLOWED ONLY where CORE A names the device — an indented bare cell, a gap inside a word, a blank turn, a layout alignment, the UI-instruction parentheses 「（次のページは…）」 — and are ASCII everywhere else; `qa_check` reports them as SOFT so they stay visible. A full-width space is NEVER replaced by ASCII spaces where the JP uses it as layout: in the proportional face an ASCII space is about a third as wide, so the device shrinks to nothing. It is replaced only inside label padding (the 0000001E rows). Every other Japanese glyph in EN output stays a HARD fail.
- PARTICLE, MODAL AND BENEFACTIVE TAILS (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #7): sentence-final particles and modals (ね, よ, さ, な, なあ, かな, だろう, のだろうか, のだ, っけ) are GRAMMAR, not words. Carry them by English mood, word order and punctuation — a question, "must", "probably", a tag question, an exclamation — and NEVER by a tacked-on phrase (", I wonder", ", you know", ", I'm sure", ", no matter what", ", too"), unless the speaker's CAST card fixes that tag for that speaker. Benefactives (てくれる / てもらう / てあげる) are ZERO in English unless the favour is the point of the line (thanks, resentment): 言ってくれる → "has said that every time", not "has said that for me every time". TEST: cover the tail; if the English sentence still says the same thing with the same force, the tail was a particle and the phrase was padding.
- IMAGES AND SET PHRASES (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #8): keep an image only where the text USES it — extends it, puns on it, a character or the narrator notices it, or it is a listed refrain or motif (CORE B3). Otherwise a Japanese idiom or set phrase is rendered by the English phrase of the same register and weight (the CORE B§4 device test applies: if nobody in the text notices the image, it is idiom, not imagery). 力の及ばないところ → "beyond my control", not "beyond the reach of my strength"; 顔を上げた → "lifted her head", not "raised her face". Verbs inside set phrases (弁を吐く, 顔を上げる, 口にする, 目を向ける) take the SET-PHRASE sense, not their literal one; 反省の弁を吐く is the newsroom formula on a teenager ("delivered a statement of remorse"), and "spat out" would add anger the JP does not have.
- FONT (2026-09-25): Latin is half-width already (PR_FONTCHANGEABLED is 0 on every message box). The shipped face ＭＳ 明朝 is fixed pitch; the patch switches the message boxes to ＭＳ Ｐ明朝 (proportional). No italics, no bold: plain text only.

## Global conventions decided 2026-09-25 (Fable, provisional and reversible; owner may overrule)
- QUOTE WRAPPERS (settles Q036/Q071/Q079): JP 「」 spoken lines → straight ASCII "..." . JP source straight double quotes "..." (text nobody present is speaking: phone messages, announcements, broadcasts, documents read on screen, a mute character's written words) → curly “...” (U+201C/U+201D, CP932-safe, render verified). JP 『』 → single '...'. Bare unquoted cells (letters, documents set without quotes) stay bare, no wrapper added. The JP distinction spoken/unspoken therefore survives as straight/curly.
- 「――」 PREFIX AND ――-ONLY CELLS (settles Q709 and the five-jobs problem): render the dash as ― (U+2015) in every job, at the same position as the JP. One mark for all jobs, as the source does. A ――-only cell stays a lone ―. Never replace it with a speaker label.
- DIALECT POLICY (settles Q611/Q785/Q843/Q070/Q149 at the policy level): NEVER an English regional or class dialect, never eye-dialect spelling, never "Engrish". Carry dialect by rhythm, blunt or soft word choice, contraction rate and fixed sentence tags per RELATIONS/CAST, plus ONE translator note at the character's first dialect line naming the dialect ("[TN: she speaks Kansai dialect]"). A dialect the JP names in narration but does not write into the lines stays that way in EN (Q843). The ～アル register: plain English plus a TN (Q149). The genteel ～ざます／～ですわ register: over-formal English, no contractions, no regional accent (Q074) — the old GLOBAL 3.9 best guess for ～ざます ("my dear", "positively") is SUPERSEDED by this line, because both are additive words.
  - THE TEST for any candidate word or spelling (rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #13): could a reader place it on a map or in a class of English speakers? Then it is BANNED. Banned by name: "Oi", "Yo", "eh", "y'all", "innit", "ya know", "my dear", "positively", "aye", "lass".
  - TAGS are pre-listed per speaker in CAST.md. A tag that is not on that speaker's list is not used; no drafter invents one.
  - ALLOWED colloquial contractions, project-wide: gonna, wanna, gotta, c'mon, dunno, 'cause. These are contractions, not dialect, and a CAST card's contraction rate decides how often they appear.
  - BANNED eye-dialect spellings: ya, yer, nothin' (and every dropped -g), wot, gimme-style respellings of a word the speaker pronounces normally.
- CALENDAR (settles Q943/Q827 provisionally): dates stay as written. Era dates keep the era name romanised ("Shiyo 805, August 1"); a Gregorian caption stays Gregorian. No conversion in either direction, no TN equating them. Reversible by a regex sweep.
- MOJIBAKE CELLS (Q872): translate the decoded intended Japanese; log as typo.
- RUBY-RESIDUE SPACING: drop the stray spaces; translate the word normally.
- NARRATIVE TENSE (Q1087, project-wide; rev. 2026-09-25, rules audit #5 — "interior" was not decidable per cell, so it is replaced by a test). JP first-person narration mixes non-past and past cell by cell; do not follow the JP tense cell by cell, and do not put a whole block in the historical present. Per narration cell, in this order:
  - (a) Put "I thought:" in front of the cell. If it still reads as the narrator's words AT THAT MOMENT — a judgment, a resolution, a ～のだ explanation, a question to herself, 分かっている, しかいない — PRESENT is allowed, as unquoted thought ("I know. Regret is not going to change anything.").
  - (b) Anything PERCEIVED, or the case at the story moment, is PAST whatever the JP tense: sensations and aches (少し身体が痛い → "my body ached a little"), the room, the weather, who is asleep, the boxes still standing in a room, a worry about tonight (風邪の心配もある → "there was the worry of catching a cold as well").
  - (c) A fact still true at the moment of telling AND stated as general (ages, kinship, a standing rule) may be PRESENT.
  - At most ONE flip between past and present per text box, unless the JP itself flips function inside the box. Exception unchanged: a block the source sets wholly in the present as a device (a dream, a running commentary) stays present and gets a DECISIONS row.
- CELL-SPLIT WORDS (Q1084): a word the JP spreads one mora per cell (「お／と／う／さ／ん？」) is spread over the SAME number of cells in English, one or two letters per cell; the English word is chosen for the cell count as much as for register, and the choice is logged in DECISIONS.
- SYLLABLE-SPLIT UNFINISHED WORDS (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1123): when the JP spreads an unfinished word one mora per cell and the word is NOT completed in the file, the cells are romanised mora by mora ("Shi... / ni... / ga...") with one TN on the first cell, and the English must not resolve into a word. The file where the JP completes the word decides the English of the completed form; the earlier romanised cells stay as they are so they remain continuable.
- TN IN ROUTE VARIANTS (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1126): a scene that exists as several near-duplicate route variants gets the same TN in EVERY variant that has the line; the player sees only one variant, so each variant is a first occurrence. "First occurrence only" counts per route, not per file.
- RUBY ATTRIBUTES (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1321): translators keep `<STYLE ID="n" RUBY="...">` byte-identical (tag parity); the compile step strips the RUBY="..." attribute from the English build, because kana furigana above an English word is meaningless. If a ruby reading carries information (a name reading, a pun), carry it in the English word or a TN.
- ELLIPSIS-ONLY CELLS (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1322, refines the audit #17 rule): a cell that is ONLY ellipsis characters stands for elapsed time or silence; render ONE "..." per JP … character (…… -> "......", 8 x … -> 24 dots). Inside sentences the collapse to a single "..." stands.
- TN POSITION ACROSS CELLS (Q1320): when the term sits in the first of two cells of one quoted speech, put the [TN] at the end of THAT cell's line, before the cell break, not after the closing quote in the next cell.
- BULLET GLYPH (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, reviewer escalation): ・ (U+30FB, CP932 0x8145) is KEPT as the bullet in English lists where the JP uses it; qa_check treats it as a kept device glyph. ELLIPSIS + MARK cells (「…………？」): the … count is kept as in ellipsis-only cells, then the mark ("............?").
- ENGLISH RUBY (owner 2026-09-25): a ruby that is only a reading aid (a hard kanji, a name reading) is DROPPED in the English build automatically. A ruby that carries meaning (a double reading, a pun, emphasis dots 圏点, a foreign word glossed by the reading) is KEPT as ruby above the English span: the drafter adds a row `jp ruby<TAB>en ruby` to patch/ruby-<id>.tsv (UTF-8, CP932-safe, short: ruby space is narrow) and keeps the `<STYLE ID="n" RUBY="jp">` tag on the English words that carry it. When the ruby is too long to fit, use a TN instead. Log each in NOTES-TL.md.
- CLOCK TIMES AND DURATIONS (Fable rulings 2026-09-25, Q1560/Q1563): a time keeps the source's own form, in digits: a 24-hour printing (a display, a timestamp) stays 24-hour ("23:50"), a 12-hour spoken form stays 12-hour ("ten at night" -> "10 at night" only when the JP uses digits; kanji spoken numbers follow the numerals rule). A duration with a unit (minutes, hours, days, years) takes digits; a count of things under ten in prose takes words.
- KEYWORD SPANS (Q1562): English words may be moved across a STYLE span boundary so that only the highlighted keyword sits in the keyword-colored span; the tag sequence stays unchanged.
- KINSHIP TERMS IN NARRATION (Q1561): the rendering fixed for a kinship term in dialogue is used in narration too, whenever the narrator refers to the person by that term.
- TN CASE 6 (Fable ruling 2026-09-25, Q1482/Q1483/Q1486): a note is also allowed for a fact every Japanese reader gets from the word itself (a memorial-year count, a naming custom, a school-year term) WHEN a character's reasoning in the scene turns on it and the English word cannot carry it. Still 12 words, still at the line end. A term that is translated descriptively gets no note (Q1482, Q1483: no TN).
- STRETCHED ENDINGS (Q1484): a stretched sound mark on a content word is carried by stretching the English word ("amaaazing"); a stretch on a grammatical ending (a copula, a particle) is dropped, since English has no vowel there.
- SUFFIXES IN DOCUMENTS (Q1485): a name inside a newspaper article, notice or message keeps its Japanese suffix exactly like speech ("-san"). No document exception, no invented "Mr./Ms." (which would also gender a person the JP does not).
- DEVICE NOTES PER FILE (Q1490): a TN that explains a device (the romanised-mora device, a dialect) is placed once per FILE at the device's first occurrence in that file; scenes are played separately.
- LEADING INDENT SPACE (Q1801, 2026-09-25): the leading full-width space (U+3000) that indents a continued cell inside one quoted turn is KEPT in English; qa_check reports it as a soft KEEPABLE line by design, no DECISIONS row needed per line.
- SPEECH-LEVEL BREAKS (Q1722, 2026-09-25): when a speaker whose card records an unbroken polite level drops to plain form in a cell, the English drops too (clipped, contraction-heavy, no polite words) for that cell only; it is an event, like a dropped suffix.
- DURATIONS, refinement (2026-09-25, section-3 review of Q1563): a duration of ONE unit in prose or speech takes the article or word, never the digit: "another hour", "about an hour later", "just this one day"; digits start at 2 ("2 hours", "3 days"). Clock times keep digits.
- IMAGE ORDER CARRIES INFORMATION (owner, 2026-09-26, soft rule for future files; do not re-edit shipped lines for it): where the JP orders its images to carry sequence or logic (「――夢の中に響く、時計の音。」: dream first, then the sound, then the waking that follows), English keeps that order even at the cost of a fronted phrase ("Echoing inside a dream, the sound of a clock.") rather than the neutral English order ("The sound of a clock, echoing inside a dream."). Apply when the order is doing work; not as a blanket inversion. SOFT: the resulting English must stay readily comprehensible on first read; if the fronted phrase reads awkwardly or ambiguously, the neutral English order wins (owner, 2026-09-26).
- SUBJECT CALQUES ARE NOT A RULE (owner, 2026-09-26, reversing the note above the same night): "the time had gone past midnight" for 時刻は深夜0時を回っていた STAYS. The project values the Japanese order and feel over 100% natural English. Only clearly unreadable stiffness is an error; a later audit collects candidate lines for the owner, nothing is enforced per line.
- INVENTED DASH (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, Q2120): an English ― (U+2015) with no ―― in the JP is allowed only where it spans a cell break inside one speech and no punctuation can carry the self-interruption; never as a replacement for a 読点. Everywhere else the JP and EN dash counts match line for line.
- ONE NOTE WORDING PER TERM (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, Q2121): a romanized term has exactly one translator-note wording project-wide (花見 = "cherry-blossom viewing"); the once-per-file rule reuses it byte for byte. A narrower sense (a drinking party) is carried by the line's own text, not by the note.
- BOARD VOICE (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, Q2122 closes Q031): the anonymous message-board posts use ONE register everywhere: the all-lowercase treatment accepted at Q1173 for 000001E1, mirrored exactly (sentence starts, "i", proper nouns) in every other board block.
- REVIEW COVERAGE (owner 2026-09-26): every line of every file, always; no sampling. Parts 1-3 re-read in full under notes/FULL-REVIEW-BRIEF.md.
- NEVER-TRANSLATE COLUMNS (2026-09-26): 閲覧年月日 (object names), 項目 of システムテキスト, 文字列名 of リンク, 場所名/出来事名/物名 of the 事典 tables (paths), any DBLocate key column.
- INVENTED DASH, refinement (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, Q2281 amends Q2120): an English ― where the JP has a 読点 or a bare topic-comment seam is allowed only when a plain comma would make a splice AND the JP is one sentence (a period would break the unit). Where a comma reads normally, it is a comma. Kept sites are listed in a DECISION row per file.
- NOTE PLACEMENT WHEN A LINE ALREADY CARRIES ONE (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, Q2280): the once-per-file note rule covers every term whose GLOSSARY row asks for a note, romanized or not; if the term's first line in the file already carries a note, the new note goes on the term's next occurrence in that file, else both notes share the first line, each as short as possible.
- INVENTED DASH, second refinement (Fable ruling 2026-09-26, part-3 follow-up): the dash is also kept where a comma would not splice but would MISREAD (a fronted name that a comma would turn into an address, when the keyword span forces the name to open the cell) and where the pause falls right after a closing quote that ends in ! or ? (American style takes no comma there).
- SPACE AT A {PAUSE} CELL BOUNDARY (owner catch 2026-09-26): when one text box is split into two cells by {PAUSE ""} and the first cell ends in sentence punctuation or a closing quote, the English first cell ends with ONE ASCII space (the engine concatenates the cells; JP needs no space after 。, English does). tools/pause_space.py sweeps it; drafters write it; qa_check will flag a missing one.
- SHOUT BRACKETS ＼ ／ (Fable ruling 2026-10-01, QP4A-03, V081): the full-width ＼ and ／ that frame a shouted word (two voices at once) are a printed device: keep them as printed around the English word, U+3000 between the voices kept. qa_check reports them SOFT.
- VOICE ANCHOR (owner 2026-10-01, METHOD.md §0): contemporary spoken English per the speaker's cast voice line, capped at the JP line's own colour; でゲス stays "gesu" (GLOSSARY row); であります = ", sir!" (GLOSSARY row).
- SCENARIO TITLES ARE COMMA-FREE (Fable 2026-10-01, part-4 titles): the EN タイトル cell feeds two comma-split .txt files (ナビデータ.txt, リファレンス概要.txt); build_navidata.py gate 2 refuses a comma. 春花と魔女と死神 = "Haruka and the Witch and the Shinigami", not "Haruka, the Witch...". Same for any 解禁編 / 閲覧年月日 / title-derived cell.
